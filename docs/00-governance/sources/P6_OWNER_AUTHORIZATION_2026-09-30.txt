12. PLANNER NOTES — NOT OWNER DECISIONS
==================================================

These are analysis only. Verify each against the repository; the
repository wins. They grant no business authority.

PN-1 Revocation linearization.
One Level-1 option:
- a command takes FOR SHARE on its actor's user row and re-reads grants
  there;
- grant, capability, role and deactivation commands take FOR UPDATE on
  the target user row.
Then revocation waits for in-flight commands, and every later command
sees it. Acting-user and target-user rows are one lock class, locked in
ascending id at that class's canonical position — never blindly first —
so two Owners changing each other's access cannot deadlock. Check this
against AZ-03 ("next request").

PN-2 command_log placement and terminal outcomes.
One Level-1 option:
- insert the command_log row first, inside the business transaction,
  keyed by command_id;
- a concurrent identical id waits on the unique key, then reads the
  committed outcome;
- because a rolled-back business transaction leaves no row, the REJECTED
  or CONFLICT terminal outcome is written durably by a separate short
  transaction after the rollback, so a replay still returns the original
  result (SF-CMD step 6).
Keep replay bound to actor and company; a failed re-authorization follows
AZ-11.

PN-3 Warnings under concurrency.
L-49 and the payment duplicate check are evaluated before commit, so two
concurrent submissions can both miss each other. A natural anchor may
exist: the project line or case for L-49, the company bank account row
for payments. Lock it, then re-check inside the transaction. FL-10
evidence lookups stay outside (H6-09). The warning and its approved
override stay exactly as approved.

PN-4 Stock = 1.
- Dispatches of the same product meet on the product guard row, then
  scope, lot, serial and reservation.
- The AVAILABLE ≥ 0 CHECK and the lot CHECK are the backstop.
- The losing dispatch fails its re-read under lock and returns REJECTED
  or CONFLICT as the SF-CMD step 4/5 mapping defines — never a partial
  commit and never a CHECK surprise as the primary path.

PN-5 Numbering.
The counter at the end of the lock order keeps its hold time minimal.
First-use period creation uses insert-or-lock. The counter's position
must stay consistent with any document-issuing AX that also locks
business anchors, including the project row when issue can change a
completion predicate.

PN-6 AX volume.
Thirty-seven sequences are manageable as one template plus a per-AX
table, with a generated acyclicity check over the statically declared
lock sets and a conformance check for the data-dependent ones. Avoid
prose per AX.

PN-7 Timeouts.
Short lock_timeout on interactive actions avoids pile-ups on shared
guard rows. Background and import actions may differ. P9 owns the
configured values.

PN-8 Password-verification cap (H6-12).
It must not create a new dependency. Options include an atomic cache
lock or semaphore with fail-closed behaviour for authentication, or a
database-backed counter, sized against the Argon2id cost that P9 tunes.
Choose one at Level 1.
