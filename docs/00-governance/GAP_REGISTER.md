# Proactive gap register

Status: REVIEW | Updated: 2026-10-04 | Owner: Planning

This is the single index of discovered gaps, risk treatment and pending owner decisions. Findings use the [owner records and supporting references](SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md#authority-and-provenance) and current P0/P1 documents. They are planning risks, not claims of vulnerabilities in an application that does not yet exist. P1 decisions and follow-up answers resolve parts of earlier gaps without proving runtime mitigation. Recommendations remain proposals unless explicitly adopted or delegated under [decision authority](CHANGE_CONTROL.md#decision-authority).

## Operating rules

Keep only meaningful failure scenarios. Every entry identifies area, evidence/description, severity, probability, impact, when it matters, mitigation, status, responsible role/decision owner and affected phase or build units. Severity expresses consequence: BLOCKER stops the named gate, CRITICAL threatens serious security/data integrity or recovery, HIGH threatens core operation, MEDIUM causes material disruption, LOW is limited. Probability is qualitative: OBSERVED, LIKELY, POSSIBLE or UNKNOWN; these are reasoned judgments, not measured incident rates.

Statuses: OPEN (unresolved); MITIGATED (evidence of reduced risk, residual risk stated); OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED (Level 2/3 choice pending); ACCEPTED_RISK (explicit authorized risk acceptance with reason and review trigger); DEFERRED (scope/dependency reason and revisit gate); CLOSED (resolution evidence). A mitigation proposal or planned test alone is not mitigation evidence. Do not accept away non-negotiable safeguards or defer a dependency beyond its build/release gate. Changing risk tolerance requires the owner.

Add findings throughout planning/execution; review at every phase end and new discovery. Move actual rules into their canonical concern, link evidence here and avoid creating a second risk register. Build-unit IDs do not yet exist; phase/domain references below must be mapped to real unit IDs in P11.

## GAP-001 — Blanket approval rules could preserve a known-bad plan

- **Description/evidence:** Original P0 change control required owner approval for every substantive technical change; source governance instructed executors to keep using the approved revision until a replacement was approved. That could preserve an unsafe revision and contradict the new delegation.
- **Impact:** Avoidable waiting, unsafe execution and provider-dependent interpretation of authority.
- **Mitigation/evidence:** Applied TECH-001: [change control](CHANGE_CONTROL.md#decision-authority) now distinguishes three levels; [lifecycle](SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md#document-lifecycle) and [feedback loop](AGENT_OPERATING_MODEL.md#executor-feedback-loop) permit direct technical correction and pause only unsafe/dependent work. No business approval was invented.
- **Owner:** Planner; no owner decision required. **Affected:** All planning phases, execution feedback. **Closure:** Document consistency check and authority examples recorded in the P0 extension review.

- **Recommended mitigation:** advice on phone locks and on signing out of Google (H7-16); the Owner's unlink and reset paths; the alert on an Owner login from a new address (LG-07). A factor kept on a separate device, or a phishing-resistant factor, would need an Owner decision.
- **Owner:** the Owner decides at the approval of the P5 authentication amendment whether to accept this residual or to ask for a mitigation; the Planner owns the P7 advice (H7-16) and the P8 tests (H8-20). **Affected:** SECURITY TM-72, AU-22, H7-16; P7, P8. **Status:** OPEN — until that decision.

## GAP-048 — Post-incident recovery can reinstate an Owner removed legitimately

- **Description/evidence:** Post-incident recovery can reinstate an Owner that was removed legitimately, in two ways that DIR-050 decided:
  - After a key compromise, the OWNER-restoration command of SECURITY AU-25 restores every pre-window Owner whose OWNER role or active flag was removed during the compromise window, as DIR-050 §3.3 step 2 decides. Nothing distinguishes the attacker's removal from one made legitimately during the window — of a co-owner who left or was removed for cause, for example. The command therefore also reverses such a removal, and the restored person, recovered through AU-14, path (e), stands as a full Owner in the review (SECURITY TM-57). The longer the window, the likelier such a removal falls inside it; an undatable leak starts the window at the leaked key's creation.
  - In Scenario B (DIR-050 §3.6), recovery runs on a restored backup or snapshot. When that backup predates the window's start, pre-window status is read at the backup's time, so an Owner removed legitimately between the backup and the window's start comes back and is recovered in the same way.
  - The amendment's final focused reviews found these (R5-02, R6-04). The amendment discloses them for the Owner's attention and claims no acceptance for them.
  - The command refuses an account touched since the incident command's closing sweep, and any touch — a RESET link included — defeats the restoration. A recovered Owner's removal of a pre-window Owner not yet restored therefore stands. To prevent this, the runbook leaves such an account untouched until its restoration and recovery, AU-12's post-incident link never goes to it, and AU-14 refuses an inactive account, so a deactivated pre-window Owner is restored before its recovery.
- **Impact:** until the other recovered Owners remove it again (CONCURRENCY_IDEMPOTENCY RV-04), the reinstated person holds every capability of the OWNER role, `permissions.manage` among them.
- **Recommended mitigation:**
  - Before the review, the runbook shows every recovered pre-window Owner the actor and time of each removal the restoration reversed and, on a restored state, the backup's time and the restored Owner set (H9-14).
  - OWNER_RESTORED is alerted (LG-07).
  - Restoring only the removals that the Owner establishes as part of the compromise would change DIR-050's rule that no pre-window Owner needs another Owner's review, and needs an Owner decision.
- **Owner:** the Owner decides at the approval of the P5 authentication amendment whether to accept this residual or to amend DIR-050 §3.3 step 2 or §3.6. The Planner owns the P8 tests (H8-21), and P9 owns the runbook (H9-14). **Affected:** SECURITY AU-25, TM-57, H8-21, H9-14; CONCURRENCY_IDEMPOTENCY RV-18, M-46; P8, P9. **Status:** OPEN — until that decision.

## GAP-049 — The first-Owner installation event has no actor rule

- **Description/evidence:** SECURITY AU-25 obliges P9 and P10 to make the installation step that creates the first Owner account write an audit event recording the OWNER assignment and the operator's identity, so that the audit trail can establish every pre-window Owner (DIR-050 §3.7). PERMISSIONS_MATRIX RG-11 allows an audit event without an application actor only for the use of an OWNER_RECOVERY link and for the operator's console commands of AU-14, AU-21 and AU-25, so the installation step's event has no actor rule yet; DIR-050 §5 lets RG-11 cover only the OWNER-restoration command. The amendment's final focused review found it (R5-11), and it is carried under DIR-050 §2 as a LOW non-material finding.
- **Impact:** without the rule P9 or P10 could find the event forbidden, or create a placeholder actor, which RG-11 forbids.
- **Recommended mitigation:** the installation step records the operator's identity as AU-14 records it — the host account and the stated name, never a user row —, and RG-11's operator rule is extended to it under P9's own authorization.
- **Owner:** the Planner, in P9 with P10 for the installation step. **Affected:** SECURITY AU-25, H9-14; PERMISSIONS_MATRIX RG-11; P9, P10. **Status:** OPEN — until P9 designs the installation step's event.

## GAP-050 — Post-incident recovery cases carried to P8 and P9

- **Description/evidence:** Three cases of the post-incident recovery of SECURITY AU-25 are carried under DIR-050 §2 as LOW non-material findings of the amendment's final focused reviews.
  - **A window start that turns out to be wrong (R6-07):**
    - Containment may use the leaked key's creation time as a conservative start, which can reach back before every OWNER assignment, or widen GAP-048. The run can never narrow that start.
    - A start later found to be earlier means an account made Owner between the true start and the recorded one has already been recovered as pre-window.
    - In Scenario B, the containment run's record is lost with the replaced state.
  - **A halt on a trusted state (R6-08 b):** "no pre-window Owner is available" sends recovery to Scenario B, so a backup is restored before the Owner's decision, though a restore cannot make a person available. A halt on a restored state already ends in the Owner's decision outside the application (AU-25).
  - **The end of recovery and its races (R7-05 c):** CONCURRENCY_IDEMPOTENCY RV-18 states no lock or transaction for recording the end of a run's recovery, and M-46 has no race between the end and a role change or an activation. A late race only equals an Owner's act after the end, so its security effect is nil.
- **Impact:**
  - a corrected start has no defined path;
  - a halt on a trusted state can lose every record since the backup without need.
- **Recommended mitigation:**
  - A corrected start — narrower, earlier or mistyped — means a new run of the incident command with that start, whose recovery follows AU-25 again. Its window spans the earlier run's recovery, so the runbook shows the recovered Owners the earlier run's decisions.
  - The established start is kept in the incident record outside the application.
  - The end of recovery is recorded in one TX-07 transaction under the OWNER role row (LK-02), which fixes the set of active Owner accounts it reads; M-46 gains that race.
  - On a trusted state, a halt goes to the Owner's incident decision, which may choose a restore. If DIR-050 §3.4's "handled as Scenario B" is read as mandating the restore, the runbook states only that the restore precedes that decision, and any change is the Owner's.
- **Owner:** the Planner — in P9 for the runbook (H9-14), and in P8 for the end's lock and the M-46 cases. **Affected:** SECURITY AU-25, H8-21, H9-14; CONCURRENCY_IDEMPOTENCY RV-18, M-46; P8, P9. **Status:** OPEN — until P9 defines the cases.
